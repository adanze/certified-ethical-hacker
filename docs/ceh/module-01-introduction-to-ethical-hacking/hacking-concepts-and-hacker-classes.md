# Hacking Concepts and Hacker Classes

## What is Hacking?

Hacking refers to **exploiting system vulnerabilities and compromising security controls** to gain unauthorized or inappropriate access to a system's resources. It also involves **modifying system or application features** to achieve a goal outside the creator's original purpose.

Hacking can be used to steal, pilfer, or redistribute intellectual property, leading to **business loss**.

### Common Network Hacking Techniques

- Creating viruses and worms
- Denial-of-Service (DoS) attacks
- Establishing unauthorized remote access via trojans or backdoors
- Creating botnets
- Packet sniffing
- Phishing
- Password cracking

### Motives Behind Hacking

Motives vary widely and may include:

- Stealing critical information or services
- Financial gain
- Thrill, curiosity, intellectual challenge
- Prestige, power, peer recognition
- Vengeance and vindictiveness
- Experimentation and knowledge

## Who is a Hacker?

A hacker is an intelligent individual with **excellent computer skills** who can create and explore computer software and hardware. Usually a skilled engineer or programmer, hackers have enough knowledge to discover vulnerabilities in a target system and typically enjoy learning the details of programming languages and computer systems.

Hackers break into systems or networks **without authorization** to destroy data, steal sensitive information, or perform malicious attacks.

- For some, hacking is a **hobby** — testing how many systems they can compromise.
- Intentions range from gaining knowledge to performing illegal activities.
- Some act with **malicious intent**: stealing business data, credit card numbers, social security numbers, or email passwords.

## Hacker Classes and Their Motivations

!!! tip "🎯 Exam-critical"

| Hacker Class | Background | Motivations | Cyber Activity | Potential Targets |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Script Kiddies** | Inexperienced; use pre-made scripts/tools without understanding them | Thrill, recognition, fun | DDoS, website defacing | Small websites, online games, forums |
| **White Hat Hackers** | Cybersecurity professionals; act with owner permission | Security improvement, salary, reputation | Penetration testing, vulnerability assessments | Corporations, government agencies |
| **Black Hat Hackers** | Extraordinary computing skills; criminal activities (also: *crackers*) | Financial gain, data theft, causing harm | Malware creation, phishing, ransomware, data breaches | Financial institutions, individuals, enterprises |
| **Gray Hat Hackers** | Operate offensively and defensively at various times | Recognition, curiosity, financial gain | Vulnerability discovery without permission, sometimes reported | Various, including high-profile organizations |
| **Hacktivists** | Politically or socially motivated individuals/groups | Promoting a cause, social justice | DDoS attacks, defacing websites, data leaks | Government sites, corporations, political groups |
| **State-Sponsored Hackers** | Highly trained professionals employed by governments | National security, espionage, political objectives | Cyber espionage, infrastructure sabotage, data theft | Other nations' government agencies, corporations |
| **Cyber Terrorists** | Extremists promoting political or religious beliefs | Spreading fear, political/ideological goals | Attacks on critical infrastructure, spreading propaganda | Critical infrastructure, public services |
| **Corporate Spies (Industrial Spies)** | Hired by companies to gather intelligence on competitors; use APTs | Financial gain, competitive advantage | Industrial espionage, data theft, spying | Competitor companies |
| **Blue Hat Hackers** | Contract-based security professionals hired before product release | Improving product security, reputation | Security audits, penetration testing | Technology companies, software firms |
| **Red Hat Hackers** | Vigilantes targeting black hats using aggressive methods | Cyber justice, disrupting malicious activities | Hacking black hat infrastructure, disabling malicious networks | Cybercriminal groups, black hat hackers |
| **Green Hat Hackers** | Newcomers eager to learn; active in online forums | Learning, curiosity, recognition | Learning hacking techniques, experimenting with simple attacks | Various, typically low-risk targets |
| **Suicide Hackers** | Unconcerned with legal consequences; act for a "cause" | Political/ideological cause | Targeting critical infrastructure | Critical infrastructure |
| **Hacker Teams** | Consortium of skilled hackers with own resources and funding | Research, tool development | Vulnerability research, planned attacks | Various |
| **Insiders** | Trusted employees with privileged/physical access to critical assets | Disgruntlement, financial gain, coercion | Bypassing security, data theft, planting backdoors | Their own organization |
| **Criminal Syndicates** | Organized criminal groups spanning multiple jurisdictions | Financial gain, money laundering | Sophisticated cyber-attacks, money laundering | Victims across jurisdictions |
| **Organized Hackers** | Hierarchical criminal groups; use rented devices, botnets, crimeware | Financial gain, selling stolen information | Network infiltration, data/IP theft, staying undetected | Various victims |
