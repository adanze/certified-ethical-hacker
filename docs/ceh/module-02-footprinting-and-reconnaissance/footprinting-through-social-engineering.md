# Footprinting through Social Engineering

## Overview

Social engineering is a **non-technical process** in which an attacker misleads a person into providing confidential information inadvertently — the target is unaware that someone is stealing confidential data. Attackers exploit the gullible nature of people and their willingness to provide information.

The process:

1. Gain the confidence of an authorized user.
2. Mislead that user into revealing confidential information.
3. Use the obtained information for malicious purposes.

## Goals and Malicious Uses

Confidential information obtained through social engineering is used for:

- Gaining unauthorized access to systems
- Identity theft
- Industrial espionage
- Network intrusion
- Fraud

## Information Gathered

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Financial / Identity | Credit card details, social security numbers |
| Credentials | Usernames and passwords |
| Infrastructure | Security products in use, OS and software versions |
| Network | IP addresses, names of servers, network layout information |
| Personal | Other personal information |

## Techniques

| Technique | Brief Description |
|---|---|
| Eavesdropping | Unauthorized interception of audio, video, or text communication |
| Shoulder surfing | Secretly observing a target's screen or keystrokes to capture credentials |
| Dumpster diving | Rummaging through trash bins to recover sensitive discarded information |
| Impersonation | Pretending to be an authorized person to trick targets into revealing information |
| Tailgating | Following an authorized person through a secured entry point without using credentials |
| Piggybacking | Entering a restricted area with the (knowing or unknowing) consent of an authorized person |
| Third-party authorization | Claiming a trusted third party (e.g., manager, vendor) has authorized the request to gain compliance |
| Reverse social engineering | Creating a problem so the target seeks the attacker's help, allowing information extraction under the guise of assistance |

## Collecting Information via Social Networking Sites

Social networking sites (LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, Pinterest, YouTube, etc.) are open to everyone, making them valuable sources of information for attackers. Attackers gather information by:

- **Browsing public profiles** — no barrier to accessing public pages.
- **Creating fake profiles** — posing as a genuine user to lure victims into revealing more information (e.g., sending a friend request; if accepted, restricted pages become accessible).
- **Collecting interests** — tracking groups and activities to mislead the victim into revealing more.

### What Attackers Get from User Activity

| What Users Do | What Attacker Gets |
|---|---|
| Maintain profile | Contact info, location, and related information |
| Connect to friends, chat | Friends list, friends' info, and related information |
| Share photos and videos | Identity of family members, interests, and related information |
| Play games, join groups | Interests |
| Create events | Activities |

### What Attackers Get from Organizational Activity

Organizations use social networking sites to connect with people, promote products, and gather feedback. Attackers exploit this too:

| What Organizations Do | What Attacker Gets |
|---|---|
| User surveys | Business strategies |
| Promote products | Product profile |
| User support | Social engineering opportunities |
| Recruitment | Platform/technology information |
| Background check to hire employees | Type of business |

## Technique Descriptions

### Eavesdropping

Eavesdropping is the act of **intercepting communication in any form** — audio, video, or text — without the consent of the communicating parties. It also includes reading confidential messages from instant messaging or fax transmissions. Attackers can gain information by tapping phone conversations or intercepting any form of communication.

### Shoulder Surfing

Shoulder surfing involves **secretly observing the target** to gain critical information. The attacker stands behind the victim and watches keystrokes or screen activity — effective for capturing passwords, PINs, security codes, account numbers, and credit card information. Crowded places make detection difficult.

### Dumpster Diving

Also known as **trashing**, dumpster diving involves rummaging through garbage bins to find sensitive information. Sources include the target company's trash bins, printer waste bins, and sticky notes at users' desks. Information gathered may include:

- Phone bills and contact information
- Financial information
- Operations-related information
- Printouts of source code or sensitive data
- Account information from ATM trash bins

### Impersonation

Impersonation involves **pretending to be a legitimate or authorized person** — a courier, janitor, technician, client, or visitor — and using phones or other communication media to mislead targets. Using this cover, an attacker may:

- Scan terminals for passwords
- Search documents on desks
- Rummage through bins
- Overhear confidential conversations or shoulder surf
